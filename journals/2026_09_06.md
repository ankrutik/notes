- #writing #pens/fountain
	- Like [[Uttam Kumar]] in [[Satyajit Ray]]'s [[Nayak (1966)]], if you have a long-unused fountain pen whose ink is not flowing, dip the tip in still or tap water for a brief moment.
	- Thick bleeding ink is not bad. Deliberately use such inks on...
		- thicker watercolor style papers
		- papers you intend not to write on the reverse side, like [[post-it]]s
- #interviews/prep [[books/Beyond Cracking The Coding Interview]]
	- Interviewers are going to matter, there are bad and good ones. You will have to judge early on in the interview and play accordingly.
	- Outcomes of interviews are arbitrary.
		- Don't be too hard on yourself if an interview goes bad.
		- Or too smug if it goes well!
	- Trap! Memorizing problems/solutions without understanding.
	- Third party recruiters
		- assume they are working for the company, not helping you
		- always establish point-of-contact with the company and deal directly
		- do not...
			- tell them about your job search
			- your current compensation and expectations
	- In house recruiters
		- contract based
		- may not be around in 6 months
	- email > text/phone
		- recruiters can pick up on how excited you are over phone
		- text replies are knee-jerk
		- stick to slow, composed email
	- demonstrating thought process is important, so is coming to an optimal solution
	- embrace the struggle during an interview, could mean that the interviewer is scaling up to your level
	- beyond staff positions, behavioral and communication skills can help more
		- explain solution before you start to code
		- practice thinking out loud while you practice problem solving
	- axiom of [[books/Beyond Cracking The Coding Interview]] : you will need to practice and you will fail some interviews
		- 2 to 6 months
	- people that do >=5 interviews, tend to do better
	- work on 2 metrics...
		- get more interviews in pipeline
		- get good at technical interviews
